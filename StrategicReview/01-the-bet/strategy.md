## The strategy in three sentences

**1. Where we place the bet.** We bet on Greece first — one named academy or private school,
football only, sold to the institution rather than the individual coach or parent, with the
athlete's phone as the surface but the club or school as the buyer; performance tracking is the
hook that earns daily opens, and the character layer (Composure, Respect, Effort, Responsibility,
Care, Transfer) is what makes an institution pay.

**2. The problem and how we create value.** Youth football runs on opinion that lives in a coach's
head, is never written down, and resets roughly twice a year when a player changes club or coach —
so ~€500 per child per year buys no persistent development record; we create value not by
*tracking* but by making the record **believable**: coach-authored, six-dimension, banded, never a
raw number, never ranked between children, GDPR-clean and owned by the player across transitions.

**3. How we know we're right.** The pilot is falsifiable on pre-set kill criteria, not on vibes —
the player-first thesis dies if under 30% of pilot players log weekly by week 8, the triangle dies
if 50% or more of coaches stop assessing after week 4, the parent role is cut if under 20% of
invited parents ever open the app — and the business thesis only clears when someone actually
pays, which has never yet happened.

### Two caveats the source docs themselves flag

- The demand evidence is coach-side and founder-side. **No 13–18 year old player has ever described
  this pain in their own words**, which is the weakest joint in a "player-first" bet.
- The pilot club or school was still unnamed — exactly the condition under which a wedge degrades
  into "build everything and hope."

## The CEO pitch: a football athlete passport

Pitching Trak as a global athlete passport that follows a young player into adult life as evidence
of their sportsmanship.

**The asset, not the app.** "Every youth sports platform sells admin software to clubs and dies
when the club churns — we issue the one thing no club can own: a coach-verified record of how a
young athlete performs *and* conducts themselves, carried by the player across every transfer from
13 to 18 and outward into adult life."

**Why it compounds.** "A child changes club or coach roughly twice a year, so today ten resets
happen between 13 and 18 and the record is lost each time; every assessment written into a Trak
Passport instead accrues to the athlete, which means the switching cost sits with the person, not
the institution, the buyer — club, school, federation — gets consistency across its coaches, and
the proud parent sharing their kid's card is our distribution."

**Why to believe us.** "It is live and authorization-hardened — we found and closed the flaw that
let a player fabricate their own assessments, because a self-issued credential is worth nothing —
and the first pilot has pre-set kill criteria, so in eight weeks you get a yes or a no, not a
story."

### Flag before taking this into a room

"Evidence of sportsmanship that follows them into adult life" collides with three of Trak's own
commitments: character is *never graded or ranked*; the claims discipline forbids implying proof of
character; and GDPR data minimisation on minors' behavioural data cuts against indefinite
retention. Pitch the Passport as a record the athlete **chooses to disclose** and re-consents to at
18 — never one a coach or employer can look up.
