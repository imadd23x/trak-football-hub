# The 90-Day Encroachment Plan

*Session: 12 September 2026. Breakout exercise: attack as the encroacher, then defend.*

Played both ways: **Veo attacks Trak**, then Trak defends. Veo is the right attacker — 6–9 month
time-to-threat, ~60% of value at risk, zero customer acquisition cost
(see the [threat board](threat-board.md)).

---

## Encroacher — Veo's 90 days to take Trak's users

**Principle: don't build Trak's product. Build the habit Trak needs, on top of hardware already
bolted to the fence.**

### Days 1–30 · Take the weekly habit

Ship **Player Moments**. The system already tags per-player clips after every upload. Monday
morning the coach gets one notification: *"Tag 3 players from Saturday."* Two taps — pick a player,
pick a tag from a fixed list. No sliders, no forms, thirty seconds for the squad. Free, on by
default, to every academy in the launch markets that already owns a camera. No sales call, no
onboarding.

*Why it wins:* Trak has to earn the coach's Monday. Veo already has it — the coach opens Veo to
watch the goals anyway.

### Days 31–60 · Take the parent, and take "believable"

Ship **Player Page**: each kid's clips plus the coach's tags over time, with a share link. Parents
already receive Veo highlights, so this lands in an inbox that already opens.

Then add the assessment — six categories, monthly — with one rule: **every rating must link to at
least one clip.**

*Why it wins:* Trak's entire claim is believability. A coach's opinion with evidence attached beats
a coach's opinion.

### Days 61–90 · Take the artifact, the schools, and the word

Ship **Season Report** — per player, auto-generated: bands over time plus a 90-second highlight
reel, built to be shared by a proud parent. Launch a schools pilot using cameras already installed.
And name it the **Player Passport** before Trak gets there.

**Price: €0**, bundled into the existing subscription. Trak's unanswered "who pays?" is answered
for them — *nobody, it's included.*

**Damage in 90 days, at zero CAC:** the coach's habit, the parent relationship, the shareable
artifact, the pricing story, and the name.

## Defender — Trak's 90 days

Trak cannot win the club in 90 days; Veo has distribution and Trak has none. So don't hold the
ground — **move to the ground the camera cannot reach and the club cannot own, before they
arrive.**

### Days 1–30 · Legal basis, label loop, named pilot

Close the legal work: entity, terms, privacy policy, versioned parental consent, and a DPIA scoped
for **both** purposes — producing the child's record *and* improving the model from it.
Lawyer-time, not engineering time, so it runs in parallel.

Ship the four-column correction capture (`source_text`, `drafted_band`, `signed_band`, `coach_id`).

**Name the pilot institution.**

### Days 31–60 · Portability, and what the camera can't see

Ship GDPR export and a player-owned passport artifact. Make leaving a club a *feature*: **change
club, keep your record.** Veo will never build this — a club-seat business cannot hand the record
to the player.

Ship Touchline to production. Phone-only, thirty seconds, works at away games with no camera, at
training, and on the moments no lens catches: took the sub without a word, clapped the player
replacing him.

> **Veo records what the ball did. Trak records what the child did.**

Close the psychologist review so the character axis can ship.

### Days 61–90 · Acceptance, not users

Get **one** institution to write into its own intake process that it reads a Trak passport — a
signature from a director of football or a school's pastoral lead. The first node of the
credential network, and the one thing Veo cannot answer by shipping software.

Answer the payer question — school or academy, per squad, priced — so "free with your camera"
isn't automatically the better offer.

## Verdict

**Veo's plan has more bite, and that is the finding.** In 90 days they take the habit, the parent
and the artifact, at the cost of one feature sprint on an installed base.

Three cracks in their plan — the whole defence:

- The record belongs to the **club that owns the camera**. Player transfers, access dies. They
  won't fix that; it's their business model.
- **No camera, no product.** Away games, training grounds, schools without budget: unreachable.
  Trak needs only a phone.
- **They won't touch minors' behavioural data.** The consent architecture and DPIA are a liability
  they have enterprise contracts to protect.

The crack in Trak's own mast: **the defence needs 90 days of work that hadn't started, funded by
revenue that doesn't exist, while the attack costs a sprint.** The legal work, the named pilot and
the export are not prerequisites to the strategy. They *are* the strategy.
