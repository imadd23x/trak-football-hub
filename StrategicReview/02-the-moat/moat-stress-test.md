# Moat Stress Test

*Session: 12 September 2026. Built on the [M1 Three-Axis Diagnostic](../01-the-bet/three-axis-diagnostic.md).*

Pull out the M1 scorecard. Find the weakest axis, then name one specific company that exploits it.

---

## Step 1 — My weakest axis: Contextual Moat (2 / 5)

Tied on the number with Data Advantage, but Contextual Moat is the true floor. Data is weak on
*one* half — proprietary is genuinely strong, compounding is zero. Moat is weak on **both**: a
shallow workflow (six sliders, minutes a week) multiplied by a switching cost of approximately
nothing. No admin gravity, no integrations, no contract, no data anyone can't walk away from.

## Step 2 — The company that exploits it: Veo

Not Hudl — Veo is the sharper knife for Trak's European launch markets. The camera is already on
the touchline at academies every weekend, already uploading, already a club habit nobody cancels.

**What they ship.** *Veo Player Report.* After a match uploads — which already happens
automatically — every player gets a card. The coach taps six ratings or dictates a line, and it is
stamped **onto the clips Veo already tagged for that player**. One share link to the parent.

**How fast.** Two quarters, conservatively. They already own the club account, the roster,
per-player clip tagging and parent sharing. What's missing is a six-field form and a share
permission. Distribution costs them nothing — the hardware is already sold in.

**Why that attack kills Trak.** The product is not the tracking, it is the *believability*. Trak's
record is words alone. Veo's would be the same words **with the footage attached** — strictly more
believable, on the one axis Trak claims to own, from inside a workflow the club cannot switch off.

**Runners-up.** **ProSoccerData** (Belgian, already selling academy player-development software
into Belgium and the Netherlands) and **Hudl** (deeper pockets, slower, more US-weighted).

---

## Where Veo won't follow

The only two things worth defending on this axis:

1. **The record belongs to the player, not the club**, so it survives a transfer that wipes a Veo
   account — but that is a promise until GDPR export exists.
2. **The character axis**, because a camera company has no reason to take on children's behavioural
   data, parental consent and a DPIA.
