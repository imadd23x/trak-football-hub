# Threat Board — who attacks, from where, with what

*Session: 12 September 2026.*

Three threat vectors. Name the attacker, estimate the timeline, quantify the damage.

| | Attacker | Vector | Time-to-threat | % of value at risk |
|---|---|---|---|:---:|
| **1. Platform** | **Google** (Workspace for Education / Classroom) | Pastoral and reflection logging native to the school's existing suite | 12–18 months | **~40%** |
| **2. Vertical** | **ProSoccerData** (Belgium) | Academy player-development platform, deeper and already sold | **Now** in BE/NL | **~50%** of the BE/NL market; ~0% in GR/CY |
| **3. Adjacent** | **Veo** | "Player Report" stapled to footage the camera already captured | 6–9 months | **~60%** |

---

## 1. Platform encroachment — Google

The obvious read is OpenAI, and it's the wrong one. A coach can already dictate "write me an
assessment of Nikos" into ChatGPT for free — but that produces a paragraph, not a *record*: no
persistence, no band vocabulary, no player view, no parent view, no signature. It commoditises the
drafting step, worth perhaps 10%, and leaves the product intact.

Google is the real one, and it attacks the place that looked safe. Schools already run Workspace
for Education. Google has already earned the education compliance posture — DPA, residency,
procurement relationship, safeguarding review — **that Trak had not started**. If pastoral and
reflection logging lands in Classroom, a school needs no separate vendor for the character half.
A regulatory moat is only a moat against companies that haven't already paid that toll.

What Google will never build: position-specific football match logging, coach assessment against
football categories, a record that follows a child *between clubs*. The football half survives.

## 2. Vertical competitor — ProSoccerData

The same thing, deeper, already selling into Belgium and the Netherlands. **Data they have that
Trak doesn't:** multi-season assessment histories across hundreds of academies — calibration, the
exact thing the flywheel analysis showed Trak can't do — plus training load, medical and
attendance data Trak has no path to.

Time-to-threat is *now*. This is the strongest argument for launching where they are absent and not
entering Belgium or the Netherlands until Trak has something they can't match.

## 3. Adjacent expansion — Veo

**Distribution advantage:** the camera is already bought, mounted and filming every weekend. Club
account, roster, per-player clip tagging and parent sharing all exist. Customer acquisition cost
for this feature: zero. They ship to an installed base; Trak ships to strangers.

It arrives with footage attached — evidence — which beats an unsupported band word on
*believability*, the single axis Trak claims as the product. Runner-up: **Spond**, free, already
inside millions of European youth teams and already holding the parent relationship. If Spond adds
a coach rating and a parent view, it reaches the entire target audience at no cost.

---

## What survives all three

1. **The record belongs to the player, not the institution.** A Veo report dies with the club
   account; a ProSoccerData profile belongs to the academy; a Classroom record ends at graduation.
   Trak's is the only one designed to survive the transfer — **a promise until GDPR export exists.**
2. **Conduct and character under school-grade compliance.** Veo won't take on minors' behavioural
   data. ProSoccerData is a performance system. Google will do pastoral generally, never through
   sport.

## The uncomfortable arithmetic

Veo's time-to-threat is 6–9 months. ProSoccerData's is zero. Trak's time-to-*sellable* — entity,
terms, privacy policy, parental consent capture, a payer decision — had not started, and it gates
revenue entirely.

**The fastest attacker moves faster than the current path to being able to take money.** Everything
on the blocked list is not admin; it is the clock.
