Pilot Scope · v2

Trak Academy Pilot

One academy, two squads, eight weeks. The pilot exists to answer a single question: will coaches keep assessing players after the novelty wears off? Everything else measured here is secondary to that.

Format  Supervised live pilot Duration  8 weeks (2 prep + 6 live) Cohort  ~30 players · 3 coaches · ~15 parents · 1 club admin Pricing  Out of scope
01  What we are testing

Five hypotheses, each with a kill condition

A pilot that can only succeed proves nothing. Each hypothesis below states in advance what result would make us change the product. H1 is load-bearing — if it fails, the others do not matter, because there is no data for players, parents, or the club to look at.

H1 · LOAD-BEARING

Coaches log without being chased

A coach will complete assessments for their squad within 48 hours of a match, week after week, without us prompting them.

Kill: assessment rate falls below 50% of matches in weeks 4–6, or only happens after a reminder. Then the product is a coach-admin burden, not a coach tool, and needs to be rebuilt around near-zero-effort capture.

H2 · PLAYER VALUE

Players understand their band unaided

A player opens their feedback, reads the band word and the coach's note, and can say what they should work on — with no adult explaining the app to them.

Kill: players cannot articulate what "Mixed" or "Steady" means about their own game, or read the band as a school grade and disengage after a low one. Then the band vocabulary needs reworking before scale.

H3 · PARENT VALUE

Parents find the summary worth having

Parents open the child summary and alerts unprompted, and say it tells them something they would not have got from the touchline or the car ride home.

Kill: parents open once and never return, or say it duplicates what the coach already tells them. Parents are the likely payer, so this changes the commercial model, not just the roadmap.

H4 · RATING CREDIBILITY

The computed band matches coach judgement

The score the engine produces from match inputs agrees with the coach's independent gut rating of that performance most of the time.

Kill: coaches disagree with the computed band on more than ~1 in 4 matches, or find a systematic bias (e.g. goalkeepers and defenders under-rated versus attackers). Then the position modifiers need recalibrating on pilot data.

H5 · CLUB OVERSIGHT

The club view surfaces something new

The academy director looks at the squad radar and finds a player, trend, or coaching gap they did not already know about.

Kill: the director says "I knew all this already." Then club oversight is a nice-to-have, not the thing an academy would buy, and the wedge is the coach or parent instead.

H6 · SECONDARY

The AI tools get used a second time

Coaches who try the session assistant or schedule parser come back to it in a later week — the test is repeat use, not first-time curiosity.

Kill: one-and-done usage across all coaches. Then the AI features are a demo asset, not a retention driver, and should not absorb further build time during the pilot.

02  Boundaries

What is in and what is deliberately not

Everything in the left column exists in the product today and will be demonstrated end to end. Everything on the right is explicitly excluded — saying so up front stops the academy from evaluating Trak against features it was never meant to have.

In scope — built and demoable

  • Four roles with role-guarded routes: player, coach, parent, club admin
  • Invite-code linking — coach codes (TRK-) and parent codes (PAR-)
  • Match logging — by the coach, on each player's behalf
  • Position-specific rating engine — GK, DEF, MID, ATT modifiers into a 7-band score
  • Coach assessments — full and quick, with a radar view
  • Player feedback, passport and evolution card
  • Recognition and awards
  • Parent home, match history and alerts
  • Coach schedule with AI text-to-fixtures parsing
  • Coach AI assistant — session plans with drill diagrams
  • Club squad analytics — band distribution, most improved, needs attention
  • Account deletion (GDPR) and data-export conversation

Out of scope — not in this pilot

  • Pricing and payments — the pilot is free; commercials are a separate conversation
  • Push notifications — not built; nudges go out by WhatsApp instead Key risk
  • Multi-academy tenancy — one organization only
  • Video, clips or footage tagging
  • Player self-logging — removed by design; every match is entered by the coach
  • Wellness check-ins — sleep, energy and mood are not part of this pilot
  • Live in-match logging — capture is post-match
  • GPS, heart rate or wearable integration
  • League, fixture-feed or federation integrations
  • Injury and medical records — wellness is subjective check-in only
  • Native iOS or Android apps — mobile web only
  • Offline capture — a live connection is required pitch-side
  • Player-to-player or club-wide messaging
  • Talent-ID scoring, benchmarking or scout-facing exports
03  The demo

Fourteen end-to-end use cases

This is the demo script. Each row is walked start to finish in front of the academy — no slides, no mock data, real accounts on real squads. Run them in order: the sequence is itself the story of a week at the academy. Budget roughly 60 minutes for UC-01 to UC-10, then a second sitting for the rest.

Run the demo silently

For UC-02, UC-03 and UC-06, hand the phone over and say nothing. Every question the person asks out loud is a design defect worth more than anything they will write on a feedback form. Note the question, do not answer it until they give up.

04  Measurement

How we will know

Targets are deliberately modest — this is a first pilot, and a target we clear easily teaches us nothing. Behavioural numbers come from in-app events; the rest come from the weekly conversations in section 05.

≥80%
Activation
Invited accounts that complete signup and link to a squad or child within 7 days.
≥70%
Match coverage
Matches played that the coach logs, measured against the fixture list.
≥60%
Assessment rate
Logged matches that receive a coach assessment within 48 hours. The H1 number.
<90s
Time to assess
Median per-player assessment time. Above two minutes, a 15-player squad becomes a chore.
≥75%
Rating agreement
Matches where the coach's blind gut band matches the computed band within one band.
≥50%
Player week-6 return
Players active in week 1 who are still opening the app in week 6.
≥40%
Parent week-6 return
Parents still opening alerts or the child summary in the final fortnight.
0
Safeguarding incidents
Wrong-child linkage, cross-squad data leakage, or any unauthorised access. Any occurrence pauses the pilot.

Instrumentation

Every behavioural number above now has a query behind it, and the scorecard is one statement: SELECT * FROM pilot_scorecard; — one row per pilot week. What remains is proving the events actually land: the app opening, a match logged with its actor, an assessment with its duration, feedback and alerts opened, the assistant used, the schedule parsed. Run the smoke test in the runbook before week 0 — a missing event cannot be backfilled once the pilot is running.

05  Feedback

What we ask, and when

Ask about last week, not about the future — people are poor predictors of their own behaviour and good reporters of it. Keep the coach call to fifteen minutes and never skip it: coach drop-off is the failure mode that arrives quietly.

Coach

15 minutes, every week, by phone
  1. Walk me through the last assessment you did — where were you, and what else were you doing at the time?
  2. Which matches did you not assess this week, and what got in the way?
  3. Before you saw Trak's band for a player, what would you have rated that performance yourself? (Ask before showing the screen — this is the H4 measurement.)
  4. Was there a rating this week you thought was plainly wrong? Which player, which match, and why?
  5. What did you do differently at training because of something you saw in Trak?
  6. Has a player or parent mentioned Trak to you unprompted? What did they say?
  7. If I switched it off tomorrow, what would you actually miss?

Player

5 minutes, in person, weeks 3 and 6
  1. Show me your last match in the app. What does that word mean about how you played? (Say nothing else. Let them read it.)
  2. Was that fair? If not, what did the app miss?
  3. What are you working on this week, and did Trak have anything to do with that?
  4. When you opened it last, what made you open it — did someone tell you to?
  5. Is there anything in here you would not want your parents to see?
  6. Would you tell a teammate at another club to use this? Say why or why not.

Parent

10 minutes, weeks 3 and 6
  1. When did you last open Trak, and what were you looking for?
  2. What did it tell you that you would not have known from the touchline or the drive home?
  3. Did anything in there change a conversation you had with your child?
  4. Was there anything you saw that worried you, or that you would rather not have seen?
  5. Are you comfortable with what the club can see about your child, and with how consent was handled?
  6. If the academy asked you to pay for this, what would your first question be? (Reaction only — do not quote a price.)

Academy director

30 minutes, week 4 and week 8
  1. Open the squad radar. Name one thing on this screen you did not already know. (The H5 test. If there is nothing, that is the finding.)
  2. Has Trak changed how you talk to your coaches about their squads?
  3. Which of your coaches took to it and which did not — and what separates them?
  4. What would you need to see before rolling this to every age group?
  5. What is the objection you would expect from your board, and what would answer it?
  6. Who at the academy would own this if we walked away tomorrow?
06  Sequence

Eight weeks

Week −2

Readiness

  • Confirm instrumentation fires end to end; without it the pilot is unmeasurable
  • Sign the data processing agreement and settle parental consent wording for minors
  • Rehearse all 14 use cases on the throwaway academy; fix anything that breaks
  • Agree the WhatsApp nudge cadence that stands in for push notifications
Week 0

Demo and setup

  • Run UC-01 to UC-14 for the director and coaches in one session
  • Set up the academy live, in the room, with the director driving
  • Issue coach codes; coaches build both rosters before leaving
  • Baseline: how does the academy assess players today, and how long does it take?
Weeks 1–2

Onboarding wave

  • Players and parents sign up and link; measure activation and where people stall
  • First full match-to-assessment-to-feedback cycle
  • Weekly coach call begins; sit in on one training session in person
  • Fix blocking bugs same-week; log everything else
Weeks 3–4

Steady state and mid-point

  • Player and parent interviews (week 3); director review (week 4)
  • Stop nudging coaches deliberately for one week — H1's real test is unprompted behaviour
  • First look at rating agreement; flag any position bias in the engine
  • Mid-point call: continue, adjust scope, or stop
Weeks 5–7

Habit or drop-off

  • No prompting at all — this is the honest retention window
  • Track whether the AI tools get a second and third use, or none (H6)
  • Freeze the feature set; only fix defects from here
Week 8

Close

  • Final interviews across all four roles
  • Score every hypothesis against its kill condition, in writing, before anyone argues
  • Hand the academy their data; confirm deletion rights and honour any requests
  • Decide: go, iterate, or stop — then open the pricing conversation
07  Risks

Known gaps to close first

These come from the current state of the codebase, not from speculation. The first two should be resolved before any real child's data enters the system.

Blocker

Minors' data and consent

The pilot processes personal data about children. Parental consent wording, a data processing agreement with the academy, a named data owner and a tested deletion path all need to be in place before week 0. Account deletion exists in the product; the paperwork around it does not yet.

Blocker

No notification layer

There is no push or email notification system. Nothing tells a coach a match needs assessing or a parent that feedback has arrived. For the pilot this is covered by manual WhatsApp nudges — but note that any engagement number achieved that way is inflated, and weeks 5–7 exist precisely to measure behaviour without it.

Built

Telemetry

The events table this depended on had never existed, so every trackEvent call was a silent no-op and five of the eight metrics below were unanswerable. Now built, with a view per metric and a single weekly scorecard query. It still fails silently for the user, so the smoke test in the runbook is a gate on week 0, not a nicety.

Confirm

Pitch-side connectivity

There is no offline capture. Coaches will assess on cold pitches with poor signal, and a failed submission after fifteen minutes of input is the fastest way to lose a coach permanently. Test on the academy's actual ground before week 0.

Watch

Thin automated test coverage

A small suite covers the rating engine and helpers. Live data from real users will find paths it does not. Keep a same-week fix loop for weeks 1–2 and expect to spend time there.

Watch

Rating engine is uncalibrated

Position modifiers were set by judgement, not fitted to data. Attacking returns carry the largest weights, so goalkeepers and defenders may band lower for equivalent performances. Capture blind coach ratings from week 1 so there is a dataset to recalibrate against.

08  Decision

What each outcome means

Go

H1 clears its target without prompting in weeks 5–7, and at least two of H2, H3 and H5 hold. Move to a paid rollout across more age groups, and build the notification layer first.

Iterate

H1 clears only with prompting, or H4 shows a systematic rating bias. The product works but the effort curve or the engine is wrong. Rebuild capture around near-zero effort, recalibrate on pilot data, and run a second pilot at another academy.

Stop

Coaches stop assessing once nudges stop, and neither parents nor the director can name something they gained. There is no wedge. Stop building, and revisit whether the buyer is the academy at all.
